## C. Delivery model

Internally you lead every engagement (scope, architecture, delivery, reporting) and add vetted engineers as work grows. The site never describes this structure: like the large firms you studied, it shows the process a client gets, not who sits behind it.

**Cadence:** kickoff and RACI · weekly status · monthly service report (retainers) · quarterly architecture review · runbooks and full handover.

**Access model:** least privilege, read-only first, MFA, client-owned accounts and repositories, NDA before discovery, DPA on request.

**AI-tool policy (new; published on the Trust page and in every SOW):**

- AI coding and writing tools are used only under business terms that exclude training on customer data.
- No client secrets, credentials or personal data are pasted into AI tools.
- Every AI-assisted change is reviewed by the engagement lead before it reaches a client environment.
- A client can opt out of AI-assisted work in its SOW.

**Continuity (new):** enterprise buyers will ask "what if you're unavailable?" The answer:

- When an engagement needs more than one person, or an Enterprise retainer is signed, a backup engineer from your references is contracted under NDA (you expect about 3 days to hire).
- Runbooks, diagrams and the credentials registry live in the client's own repo and vault, so anyone authorised can pick up the work.
- The Trust page mentions continuity cover only once a backup engineer is actually contracted.

**Capacity limit:** solo, take at most 3 Professional retainers plus 1 project at a time. Hire or contract the first engineer when you pass about 70% of that load for a month. An Enterprise retainer brings the backup engineer in from its first day.

**Approved wording:**

- "Every engagement runs on a written SOW, a kickoff, weekly status reports and a monthly service review."
- "You get documented runbooks, architecture diagrams and a full handover. No lock-in."
- "We work in your accounts and repositories with least-privilege access and MFA, with an NDA available before discovery."
- "As your scope grows, delivery scales with vetted engineers working to the same standards and access controls."

**Banned wording (enforced by the content lint):** "direct access to senior engineers", "our senior engineering team", "on-call engineers 24/7", any headcount figure, "I", "gig", "hourly", "packages from", "guaranteed uptime", "zero downtime", "zero data loss", "100%", "certified" (until held), "Ltd", "Inc" or "LLC" (until registered), "our offices", "local LLM", "on-premise LLM", "fine-tuned model", published clock hours, "PM-led", "project-manager-led" and "one accountable lead".

## E. Proof and trust

Your past-project % results stay on the site as they are. Testimonials and client logos appear only when they are real; the components are built and switch on automatically.

**Why the evidence matters:** the UK CAP Code requires documentary evidence before any objective claim is published (rule 3.7) and proof that a testimonial is genuine, with the giver's contact details (rule 3.45). The FTC's rule on testimonials allows penalties of up to $53,088 per violation, with warning letters sent since December 2025. Real results with records pass both; the practical test is a buyer asking "which client was that?" on a call.

### Rules for the % results

1. **Evidence later (your decision):** collect screenshots, dashboards or reports for each number over time. Until then, be ready to describe the project behind any number when a buyer asks on a call.
2. **A result, not a promise:** "cut deployment time by 50% for a SaaS team" rather than "50% faster deployments".
3. **Honest credit:** your earlier freelance DevOps work is shown as "delivered by our lead architect", not as ZanamTech clients.
4. **Recommended: drop "guarantee".** 99.9% stays as availability achieved; the words "SLA guarantee", "zero downtime" and "zero data loss" go, because a guarantee on infrastructure you don't own becomes a contract liability.
5. **Testimonials are real or absent.** No testimonials written by ZanamTech. The section stays hidden until the first real one arrives.
